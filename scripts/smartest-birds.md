# The Smartest Birds on Earth, Ranked — Video Script

Oct 20, 2026 · @sokphaladam

## Overview

A 10-11 minute countdown video, #10 to #1, about 1,500 words of voiceover at a calm documentary pace. Each bird gets roughly 50-60 seconds: the hook fact, the experiment that proved it, and one "wow" line. The jackdaw appears at #8 as a nod to the channel name.

| Rank | Bird | Proof of intelligence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | Green heron | Uses bait to fish |
| 9 | Pigeon | Tells Monet from Picasso, spots cancer in scans |
| 8 | Jackdaw | Reads human eyes and gaze |
| 7 | Eurasian magpie | Recognised itself in a mirror |
| 6 | Kea | Solves puzzles, judges probability |
| 5 | Goffin's cockatoo | Picks 5-step locks, carries tool kits |
| 4 | Rook | Solved the "Aesop's fable" water test |
| 3 | African grey parrot | Alex learned colours, shapes, numbers |
| 2 | Common raven | Plans for tomorrow, trades with humans |
| 1 | New Caledonian crow | Builds hooks and multi-part tools |

Facts are from well-known published studies but written from memory; double-check names and years before publishing.

| 2:30 | Jackdaw close-up, pale silver eyes | "#8 JACKDAW" | Number eight is the bird this channel is named after: the jackdaw. |
| 2:38 | Person looking at a cup, jackdaw following the gaze | "It reads your eyes" | Jackdaws watch where your eyes are looking. In experiments, they used a human's gaze to find hidden food. |
| 2:58 | Pair of jackdaws preening each other | "Partners for life" | They also stay with one partner for life and share food with them. Their pale eyes may even help them communicate with each other. |
| 3:20 | Magpie on a fence | "#7 EURASIAN MAGPIE" | Number seven: the Eurasian magpie. |
| 3:26 | Magpie in front of a mirror, coloured mark on its neck | "The mirror test" | Scientists put a coloured mark on magpies' necks, where they could only see it in a mirror. Some magpies tried to remove the mark. |
| 3:46 | Graphic: elephant, dolphin, ape, magpie | "Rare club" | That suggests they know the bird in the mirror is themselves, a skill linked to apes, dolphins and elephants. Scientists still debate the results, but it made history. |
| 4:10 | Green parrot in snowy mountains | "#6 KEA" | Number six: the kea, a mountain parrot from New Zealand. |
| 4:16 | Kea pulling rubber from a car, opening a bin | "The troublemaker" | Kea are famous for opening rubbish bins and pulling apart cars. Some were even filmed moving traffic cones on the road. |
| 4:36 | Kea choosing between two jars of tokens | "It understands odds" | In one test, kea chose the jar most likely to give them a reward. That is a basic kind of probability, something human babies only start to show around their first year. |
